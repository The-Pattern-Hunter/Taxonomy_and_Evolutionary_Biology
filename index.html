About This Book

Every living thing carries two kinds of evidence: the pattern of its body, and the record of how that pattern came to be. Pattern and Descent is an eleven-chapter textbook for the NEP-2020 sixth-semester BSc Zoology syllabus, moving from the logic of classification through the origin and fossil record of life to the evolutionary forces of selection, drift, migration, and mutation that shape descent.

The chapters work through real material: identifying a Simlipal ground beetle with a dichotomous key, tracing catfish populations across the Hirakud reservoir with the Hardy–Weinberg equation and a chi-square test, reading Hamilton's rule in the kin selection of eusocial insects, and following the Irrawaddy dolphin of Chilika lagoon and the human lineage itself through phylogenetic reconstruction. Seventy-two original diagrams, worked examples, common misconceptions, and a nine-practical laboratory companion accompany the text throughout.
